Status: final synthesis of the three proposals (rjtbd-first, progress-first, service-first) and the three judgments (conversion, compliance, transcreation), 2026-09-04. This is the spec the rewrite follows. Skeleton: rjtbd-first (tokenised facts, the right-job spine, explicit forbidden uses of "deserving"). Hero form: progress-first before-to-after. Grafts: service-first's five questions (no ladder, rung or climb words), the working-room hero image, the answers-first day-3 email. ## 1. One-minute summary for the owner

The site says one thing: one real decision you already carry, worked across the six months until the people who depend on it can follow the reasoning, with the method kept. That is the progress people are buying: a change in how a working manager decides, not a certificate, a fee or a schedule. Every page opens on that change, shows the means as facts from one source, and keeps the boundaries in the same view: what the certificate is and is not, and who decides the fee, the scholarship and employer funding. Hope is written as what the participant does during the six months, never as what happens to them afterwards. The team serves the reader's decision first, including an honest no.

## 2. The change of experience

Progress is a change of experience, and experience is what the reader does in a working week. Both states below are activities the participant performs; neither is an outcome.

- From-state: the reasoning for a consequential decision lives in one head. It is defended in meetings rather than shown. The people who must act on it are waiting for a decision. The manager cannot step out of the role to learn how to lead it.
- To-state (reached during the six months, inside the reader's own organisation): the same decision is defined as a problem, tested against the frameworks and a coach, written as a plan with decisions, actions, owners and measures, submitted for faculty review, and explained to the people who act on it. The frameworks and working templates stay with the participant.
- Never written as the to-state: promoted, more confident, a business that runs itself, a team that trusts you, funding secured, a title. Test every after-state: could a graduate read it back and say "that is what I did", not "that is what I was promised"?
- The before-state is honoured, never diagnosed: "Instinct and experience carried you here" is the base; "your toolkit is inadequate", "outgrown", "decide badly" and "faster than you" are banned.

## 3. The right job to be done, and how the good thing is handled

The right job: to make the next consequential decision they are responsible for in a way they can examine, explain and lead other people through, without leaving the role that produced it. The programme is hired for that job and nothing larger: one live issue in the participant's own responsibility, across {FACTS.durationLong}, through {FACTS.trainingDays} training days across {FACTS.liveSessions} scheduled sessions, one-to-one coaching and an applied business project, to a written action plan reviewed by faculty, with the method kept.

The good things are the jobs the reader arrives with (a credential to add, a fee to minimise, a schedule that fits, carrying on alone) and the site's own easy sells (free, six months, no exam, claimable, scholarship, CMI, AI, fits your calendar). They are handled without disparagement by six rules:

1. The good thing is only ever the reader's own motive or the site's own claim. It is never another provider, an academic MBA, a short course, attendance-based training, an HRD Corp provider or an AI vendor.
2. The good thing lives in this writers' table, not on the page. The on-page "good things" panel is rejected; no card may be titled with a price, speed or credential cue.
3. On the page the good thing appears only as a term stated beside the right thing: the fee with who decides it; the duration as a fact in the strip; the assessment as a presence (a written plan reviewed by faculty), never an absence ("no exam").
4. Other routes serve a different job. Define the comparison group ({COMPARISON_SCOPE} adjacent to every table), add a first row "Built for", and name the academic MBA as the right route when the reader's purpose needs an academic degree.
5. "The shortcut cuts" is said only about methods (benchmarking, fixing the symptom, buying the tool first) and about the site's own claims. Never "not a shortcut", "unlike other programmes", "faster than", "not just attendance".
6. The reader is never lectured on their priorities. "Credential, fee and schedule come second" and "price is not the first question" are banned; the page simply opens on the change and states the terms.

## 4. Hope without promises

Hope is the credible availability of a change, not a forecast of its consequences. Rules:

- The after-state is an artefact or an act that occurs during the six months (a written plan, reasoning others can review, frameworks retained), never a status conferred afterwards.
- "You will" only for what the programme controls (hold the fee in writing, submit the plan for faculty review, receive the {CERTIFICATE_POSITIONING.credential} on successful completion). Capability intent is "designed to", with the disclaimer adjacent: the method develops decision capabilities; business outcomes depend on the participant's evidence, choices and execution.
- Evidence carries hope: counts attributed to ABC and dated, published schedules, a catch-up rule recorded in writing. No derived figures (completion or satisfaction rates).
- Hope near dates is never scarcity: "Open for enquiries" is the only availability wording; no seat counts, countdowns, "still open" or "last chance". Hope near money is never hope for an award: {FACTS.scholarshipEligibility} verbatim, HRD Corp decides.
- The honest no is part of hope: a conversation that can end in "not the right fit" is what makes the yes trustworthy. "Hope" is never used pejoratively ("a decision rather than a hope" is banned).
- Wherever the certificate or the word "progress" appears, {CERTIFICATE_POSITIONING.professionalRelevance} renders in the same block.

## 6. Servant leadership

Posture, never a badge. The maxim renders as "Real leadership is servant leadership: it is measured by the people it serves." (the owner's wording contains an audit-banned word and is never placed in scanned copy). The term may be named once on the site, on /how-it-works beside {FLOW} or in the guide's introduction, and in Malay and Chinese it follows the local-term (English) rule.

Where it lives: the hero eyebrow and audience line (who the decision is for); /curriculum (owners and measures are how a leader makes the plan usable by others); /faculty (what practitioners and coaches do for the participant's project); /how-it-works ("Right Questions" is where the question that serves the people affected is chosen); the employer route (developing a manager responsible for a team); /about (the coordinator's purpose, including telling you when the programme is not the right fit); insights (systems thinking takes responsibility for the structure; design thinking begins with the person you are trying to serve); the emails and the assistant (answers first, then the ask); the enquiry section (what the team gives before what it asks).

Where it is barred: /fees, /hrd-corp-claimable, the scholarship self-check, any scholarship or HRD Corp sentence in any language; any sentence that contains "deserve" together with fee, scholarship, HRD Corp or admission (the panel rejects "deserves a conversation" beside the fee); any outcome for the people served (trust, results, a built leadership bench); "we practise servant leadership" or "the curriculum teaches servant leadership" (ABC has not confirmed the term as curriculum). Permitted "deserving": the team earns the conversation by answering first; the leader earns the decision by building a plan that serves the people who act on it; a product deserves the customer by serving the job the customer needs done.

## 7. Narrative spine (hero to post-enquiry email)

1. Name the change: hero H1 in before-to-after form.
2. Give it a concrete form: the lede (one live issue worked into a plan the team can act on; the method stays).
3. State the means and the boundaries in the same viewport: {ABC_PROFILE.programmePositioning}; days and sessions; fee, dates and employer-led funding; "An enquiry does not commit you to enrol or pay."
4. Turn the pressures to hope: {FORCES} restated as "a leader can still" plus an activity.
5. Name who it is for: the audience line and {PROGRAMME_AUDIENCE}, and the people waiting on those decisions.
6. Show the working room: a training day from the participant's seat, the between-session weeks, one-to-one coaching, peers carrying decisions of similar weight.
7. The five questions a working manager settles for other people (5S order, no labels).
8. Recognition as evidence with the boundary: {CERTIFICATE_POSITIONING.distinction} {CERTIFICATE_POSITIONING.professionalRelevance} and Chartered Manager as a separate optional CMI route.
9. Proof attributed to ABC and dated: {PROGRAMME_PROOF}.
10. Safety in writing: /fees, /hrd-corp-claimable, /intakes, /faq, refund terms, each with the decision-maker named.
11. Fit in both directions: the Programme fit check, the programme guide, the comparison page, "not the right fit if".
12. The enquiry is about the decision you named: enquiry section, the optional one-line field, the WhatsApp prefill.
13. Afterwards the promise is kept: the acknowledgement honours the preference; day 3 answers the three fit questions; day 7 gives the published dates; day 14 hands the decision back.

## 13. Transcreation notes for Malay and Chinese
